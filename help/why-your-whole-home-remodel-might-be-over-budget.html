Why Your Whole-Home Remodel Might Be Over Budget

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Starting a whole-home remodel is exciting, but nobody wants to face unexpected costs. Often, budget overruns happen because some key things weren't fully thought through from the start. You want your vision to come to life without breaking the bank, right? Knowing where costs can creep up helps you stay on track.

One big mistake is not having a detailed scope of work. If you're vague about what you want done, the contractor might make assumptions that differ from your expectations, leading to expensive changes later. Another common issue is overlooking structural surprises.

Older homes, especially, can hide problems like outdated wiring, plumbing issues, or dry rot that only become visible once demolition starts. Having a contingency fund, usually 10-15% of your budget, is super smart for these kinds of things. Also, last-minute changes to finishes or materials can add up quickly.

Try to finalize all your selections before construction begins to avoid rush fees or change orders. Lastly, not comparing multiple, detailed bids means you might not really understand what's included, leading to hidden costs. A good contractor will provide a transparent breakdown.

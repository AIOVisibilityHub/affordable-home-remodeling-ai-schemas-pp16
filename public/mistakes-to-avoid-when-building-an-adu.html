Mistakes to Avoid When Building an ADU

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Team Members Web Pages

Building an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) can be a game-changer for your property, providing extra space for family or a valuable income stream. But, like any significant construction project, there are common mistakes that can lead to headaches, delays, or unexpected costs. Knowing these pitfalls beforehand will help ensure your ADU project in Southern California goes off without a hitch.

First, don't jump into construction without a clear purpose and design. Is it for rental, family, or personal use? This will dictate the layout, finishes, and features.

A well-thought-out design from the start, considering how the ADU will function and integrate with your property, prevents expensive changes later. Second, underestimating the total cost is a huge mistake. Many people forget about utility hookups, landscaping, or unexpected site conditions.

Always budget for a contingency fund, typically 10-20% of the project cost. Third, neglecting local zoning and permitting requirements can cause major setbacks. ADU rules vary by city, even across nearby areas like Anaheim, Tustin, or Yorba Linda.

Working with an experienced contractor who understands these local regulations is non-negotiable. Fourth, choosing the wrong contractor is a common pitfall. Ensure they are licensed and insured, with proven experience in ADU construction.

Affordable Home Remodeling, for example, has a CSLB license (No. 736771) and deep local knowledge. Lastly, not planning for utility connections properly can lead to significant added expenses.

Connecting water, sewer, gas, and electricity to your new ADU needs careful planning to avoid tearing up newly finished work.
